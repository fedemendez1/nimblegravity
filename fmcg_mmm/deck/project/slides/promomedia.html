Discount = volume × (shelf price − price paid), before any retailer funding. Source: client data, marketing mix model.

Discount given away = volume x (shelf price - price actually paid): £2.8m in 2022, £10.1m in 2023, £14.9m in 2024 (8% of revenue at shelf price). Promoted share of volume went from 16% to 33%. The model finds almost no extra volume from promotions: about +1.2% of 2024 volume, worth ~£2m, so ~£0.13 back per £1 of discount (likely range: nothing to £0.42). Scenario, back to 2022 promotion levels on 2024 volumes: discounts fall by ~£11m, volume revenue lost ~£1.5m (at worst ~£4.8m), net +£6m to +£10m a year. Why we believe it: the model measures promotion depth x breadth week by week, controlling for price, distribution and weather, and finds +1.7% volume per +10 points of promotion intensity (likely range -1.9% to +5.3%): small and not clearly different from zero. Consistent with that, promoted share doubled while total volume stayed flat. Limits: weekly national data cannot see stockpiling or within-week shifts, and promotion intensity rose over time alongside price, so its effect is measured with some noise. Caveats: we do not know who funds the discounts (brand vs retailer) or the trade terms; promotions may be part of what secured the 2024 listings, so cut gradually and watch distribution; long-term brand effects are not measured; revenue, not profit.
