The message: promotions doubled but bring almost no incremental volume, so most of the discount is money given away. Discounts given away (volume x (shelf price - price paid)): £2.8m in 2022, £10.1m in 2023, £14.9m in 2024, 8% of revenue at shelf price. Promoted share of volume went from 16% to 33%. The model finds almost no incremental volume from promotions: about +1.2% of 2024 volume, ~£2m, so a promotion ROI of ~0.13 (likely range nothing to £0.42), against ~1.06 for media, computed the same way. Scenario, back to 2022 promotion levels on 2024 volumes: discounts fall by ~£11m, incremental revenue lost ~£1.5m (at worst ~£4.8m), net +£6m to +£10m a year. Cut the biggest discounts first: they give away the most money per kg. Evidence: promotion depth x breadth is measured week by week, controlling for price, distribution and weather: +1.7% volume per +10 points of intensity (range -1.9% to +5.3%), not clearly different from zero; promoted share doubled while total volume stayed flat. Limits: weekly national data cannot see stockpiling; we do not know who funds the discounts or the trade terms; promotions may have helped secure the 2024 listings, so watch distribution as they come down. Revenue, not profit. On-slide footnote (removed): Discount = volume × (shelf price − price paid), before any retailer funding. Source: client data, marketing mix model.
