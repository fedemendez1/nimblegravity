Shoppers have accepted our price rises so far

Price elasticity

−0.5

If our price rises 1% vs competitors, we sell 0.5% less

Volume falls by less than price rises, so each increase has added revenue. That is how revenue grew 32%.

  • We measured this while our price was 30% to 110% above competitors'
  • Today we are 95% above them, near the top of that range. Beyond it, shoppers may react more strongly

Hold the price gap. Before any further rise, test +5–8% at one retailer for 12–16 weeks, with the other retailers as control.

Elasticity −0.47 (90% range −0.56 to −0.38), stable across every model version we tested (−0.41 to −0.50). Inelastic: a 1% higher price vs competitors loses about 0.5% of volume, so revenue still rises. Important: this is the elasticity over the prices we have observed (1.3x to 2.1x competitors). Elasticity usually rises as the gap widens, and bottled water is easy to substitute (other brands, private label, tap). We are at the top of the observed range, so the history cannot tell us how shoppers would react to more. Hence: hold the gap and learn with a controlled test before any further increase. Test design: shelf price +5-8% at one mid-size retailer, 12-16 weeks spanning spring into summer, other retailers as control (difference-in-differences). Read volume, revenue and switching to competitors and private label. UK grocers price nationally, so a pure regional test is not practical; the retailer is the test region. Size: at -0.5, +5% means about -2.5% volume against ~5% weekly noise, hence +8% or 16 weeks to read it clearly. Needs sales by retailer (EPOS or Nielsen/Circana).
